# Leadership & Staff Engineering

The technical-leadership knowledge ladder: mentoring, cross-team influence, leading large-scale technical change, technical-debt advocacy, and design-review/RFC process design — each treated as a working engineering skill in its own right, distinct from its interview-narration counterpart in `syllabus/20-interview-preparation/behavioral/`. New topics in this domain are assigned IDs in the plan's reserved `T-1900`–`T-1999` range (§9).

> **Phase 5 update (2026-09-04).** Per the Section 2.7 decision (approved 2026-09-03), this domain *references* — never duplicates — five existing `20-interview-preparation/behavioral/` chapters (07 Mentoring, 09 Cross-Team Influence, 10 Migrations, 11 Technical Debt Advocacy, 12 Design Reviews and RFCs) as source material for a new Level 1–4 knowledge ladder. Each behavioral chapter is exclusively STAR-interview-narration content (verified by reading all five in full before writing); none of them cover the underlying working skill itself — how to actually run a 1:1, build a stakeholder map, sequence a migration, quantify debt, or design a review process. That gap is what this domain's five new chapters fill. Two chapters (Leading Migrations, Technical Debt) also draw an explicit boundary against existing `17-architecture` chapters that own the architectural/technical mechanics of the same subject; one chapter (Design Reviews and RFCs) draws a three-way boundary against both the ADR-writing chapter in `18-engineering-practices` and the verbal-narration chapter in `20-interview-preparation/technical-answers`. All boundaries are stated explicitly in each new chapter's own opening section, not left implicit.

> **Gap found and closed: Incident Command (2026-09-11).** A full repository-wide 22-domain gap audit found this domain — despite its own original plan being complete — had no coverage of real-time, during-incident coordination structure, distinct from both [Incident Response and Blameless Postmortems](../13-observability/incident-response-and-blameless-postmortems.md)'s post-incident focus and [Production Incident Narratives](../20-interview-preparation/behavioral/04-production-incident-narratives.md)'s interview-narration focus. Closed with [Incident Command: Roles and Real-Time Coordination](incident-command-roles-and-real-time-coordination.md) (T-1906, additive beyond this domain's own closed five-topic plan), built directly on Google's own SRE book chapter on managing incidents (verified live via `WebFetch` before citing) — the real four-role structure (Incident Commander, Ops Lead, Communications, Planning) and the real, explicit principle that the operations team should be the only group modifying the system during an incident, i.e., the IC coordinates and does not personally debug.
>
> **Gap found and closed: Hiring and Team Building (2026-09-11, same audit) — this domain's audit gaps are now fully closed.** The second item the same audit named for this domain: no coverage of hiring as a structured engineering-adjacent skill, or of team composition as a deliberate, ongoing Staff-level responsibility. Closed with [Hiring and Team Building](hiring-and-team-building.md) (T-1907, additive beyond this domain's own closed five-topic plan), built on Google's own published re:Work research (verified live via `WebFetch` before citing) that structured interviewing is measurably more predictive of job performance than unstructured interviewing, plus the well-established anchoring-bias risk in group debriefs and the real, structural mentoring-capacity limit that bounds how fast a team's junior headcount can healthily grow relative to its senior capacity.
