> **Gap found and closed: Estimation and Story Points (2026-09-21).** A follow-up gap audit found [SDLC and Agile Methodology Fundamentals](sdlc-and-agile-methodology-fundamentals.md) covers Scrum's artifacts, roles, and sprint cadence in depth, but had zero coverage of estimation itself — story points, velocity, and how both get misused organizationally. Closed with [Estimation and Story Points: Relative Sizing, Velocity, and Its Misuses](estimation-and-story-points.md) (T-1805), backed by a real, fully deterministic Java demo (`practice/java/engineering-practices/estimation-and-story-points/`) proving numerically, not just by assertion, that comparing velocity across two teams is invalid: two teams estimating the identical 135 hours of real work, under two different (each internally consistent) point-per-hour calibrations, produce real velocities of 61 and 127 points respectively — a real 2.1x difference for delivering exactly the same work.
>
> **Gap found and closed: Sprint Retrospectives (2026-09-21, same day).** A second follow-up pass found [SDLC and Agile Methodology Fundamentals](sdlc-and-agile-methodology-fundamentals.md) names the sprint retrospective three separate times (as a Scrum ceremony, a common-mistakes note, and an interview answer) without ever explaining what actually happens in one or the specific, common way retrospectives fail.
