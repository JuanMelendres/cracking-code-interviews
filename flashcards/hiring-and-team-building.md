# Flashcards: Hiring and Team Building

**Canonical chapter:** [`syllabus/19-leadership-staff/hiring-and-team-building.md`](../syllabus/19-leadership-staff/hiring-and-team-building.md)

## Card: Why a shared rubric matters more than shared questions

**Prompt:**
Two interviewers ask the identical question but each privately decides what a "good" answer looks like. Is that structured interviewing?

**Answer:**
No — a shared question list alone still produces inconsistent, poorly-calibrated signal. The rubric itself (what earns a 4 vs. a 2 on this exact question) needs to be written down and agreed upon before interviews start.

**Why it matters:**
The distinction between "we ask the same questions" and genuine structured interviewing, which requires the shared rubric too.

**Common trap:**
Believing a shared question bank alone is sufficient for consistent, comparable hiring signal.

## Card: Fixing anchoring bias in debriefs

**Prompt:**
How do you prevent the first person to speak in a hiring debrief from swaying the whole group's assessment?

**Answer:**
Require every interviewer to submit an independent, written assessment before any group discussion begins — exactly what structured interviewing's per-interviewer rubric scoring already produces as a natural byproduct.

**Why it matters:**
Anchoring bias is a well-documented, real risk in any group decision, not unique to hiring.

**Common trap:**
Running an open debrief discussion first and treating independent scoring as an optional afterthought.

## Card: Culture add vs. culture fit

**Prompt:**
What's the real difference between hiring for "culture fit" and hiring for "culture add"?

**Answer:**
"Fit" hires someone who resembles the team's existing composition and style, systematically narrowing perspective over time. "Add" hires someone bringing a genuinely different, currently-missing strength, actually growing the team's collective capability.

**Why it matters:**
A real, important distinction often used interchangeably in casual hiring conversation.

**Common trap:**
Treating "good culture fit" as an unambiguously positive hiring signal without asking what it might be narrowing.
