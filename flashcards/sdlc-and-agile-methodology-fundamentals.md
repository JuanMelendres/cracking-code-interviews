# Flashcards: SDLC and Agile Methodology Fundamentals

**Canonical chapter:** [`syllabus/18-engineering-practices/sdlc-and-agile-methodology-fundamentals.md`](../syllabus/18-engineering-practices/sdlc-and-agile-methodology-fundamentals.md)

## Card: The 6 SDLC phases

**Prompt:**
Name the 6 phases of the Software Development Life Cycle, in order.

**Answer:**
Requirements, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment, Maintenance.

**Why it matters:**
Shared vocabulary across every process model — Waterfall and Agile are just different answers to how you move through these same phases.

**Common trap:**
Naming Agile ceremonies (standup, retrospective) as if they were SDLC phases themselves.

[SDLC and Agile Methodology Fundamentals](../syllabus/18-engineering-practices/sdlc-and-agile-methodology-fundamentals.md)

## Card: Waterfall vs. Agile

**Prompt:**
What's the real, mechanical difference between Waterfall and Agile — not just "old" vs. "modern"?

**Answer:**
Waterfall runs through all 6 SDLC phases once, strictly in order. Agile runs through the same 6 phases repeatedly in short sprints, each producing a working increment — shortening the feedback loop so a wrong assumption is caught in weeks, not months.

**Why it matters:**
The real mechanism (feedback-loop length) is what an interviewer is actually testing for, not just knowing the two names.

**Common trap:**
Describing Agile as "no planning" rather than "planning in short, repeated cycles."

## Card: Scrum vs. Agile

**Prompt:**
Is Scrum the same thing as Agile?

**Answer:**
No — Agile is the broader philosophy (the 2001 Manifesto's four values). Scrum is one specific, widely used framework for practicing it, with defined roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team), artifacts (Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment), and ceremonies. Kanban is a real, different Agile framework.

**Why it matters:**
Common real confusion — treating the two as fully interchangeable is an imprecise answer.

**Common trap:**
Assuming any team that isn't doing Scrum "isn't Agile."

**Related:**
[SDLC and Agile Methodology Fundamentals](../syllabus/18-engineering-practices/sdlc-and-agile-methodology-fundamentals.md)

## Card: Scrum's core artifacts

**Prompt:**
Name Scrum's three core artifacts.

**Answer:**
Product Backlog (everything that might be built, prioritized), Sprint Backlog (the subset pulled into the current sprint), and the Increment (the real, working software produced by the sprint).

**Why it matters:**
A precise, concrete answer beats a vague "they track their work in a backlog."

**Common trap:**
Confusing the Product Backlog (the whole list) with the Sprint Backlog (this sprint's slice).

[SDLC and Agile Methodology Fundamentals](../syllabus/18-engineering-practices/sdlc-and-agile-methodology-fundamentals.md)

## Card: When Waterfall is still the right choice

**Prompt:**
Is Waterfall simply obsolete? Give a real context where it's still defensible.

**Answer:**
No. Waterfall remains defensible when requirements are genuinely stable and well-understood up front and the cost of late-discovered change is very high — certain regulated, safety-critical, or fixed-contract engagements.

**Why it matters:**
A Senior-level answer distinguishes "usually not the best default" from "always wrong" — process-model choice is a real trade-off, not a fashion judgment.

**Common trap:**
Treating Waterfall as universally bad rather than context-dependent.
