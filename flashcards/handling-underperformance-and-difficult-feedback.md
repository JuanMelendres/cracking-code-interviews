# Flashcards: Handling Underperformance and Difficult Feedback Conversations

**Canonical chapter:** [`syllabus/19-leadership-staff/handling-underperformance-and-difficult-feedback.md`](../syllabus/19-leadership-staff/handling-underperformance-and-difficult-feedback.md)

## Card: Pattern vs. single instance

**Prompt:**
How do you distinguish a genuine performance problem from a temporary rough patch?

**Answer:**
A genuine performance problem is a recurring pattern — the same specific issue across multiple, distinct instances. A single bad sprint is not grounds for a formal performance conversation; nearly everyone has one occasionally.

**Why it matters:**
Over-reacting to normal variation and under-reacting to a real pattern are both real, common mistakes.

**Common trap:**
Treating any single missed deadline as grounds for escalation.

## Card: Observation vs. interpretation

**Prompt:**
Why should difficult feedback lead with an observation rather than an interpretation?

**Answer:**
"You missed the deadline" (observation) is a fact both people can agree on. "You don't care about your commitments" (interpretation) is a story about why, and it's often wrong or incomplete — the real cause might be an unclear requirement, a personal circumstance, or something else entirely. Leading with the observation and asking what happened keeps the conversation a shared diagnosis instead of an accusation.

**Why it matters:**
This is the real mechanism that determines whether a difficult conversation lands constructively or triggers defensiveness.

**Common trap:**
Asserting the reason behind a performance problem before actually asking the person what happened.

## Card: Why avoidance is the costliest mistake

**Prompt:**
What's the single most common and most costly mistake in handling underperformance?

**Answer:**
Avoiding the conversation entirely, hoping the problem resolves itself. It doesn't — it either continues quietly damaging team output and morale, or eventually forces a much harder, later intervention that a timely conversation could have prevented.

**Why it matters:**
There's no version of this that avoids discomfort entirely — the real choice is a smaller, earlier discomfort versus a larger, later one.

**Common trap:**
Waiting for a scheduled quarterly review instead of addressing a pattern promptly while the specifics are still fresh.
