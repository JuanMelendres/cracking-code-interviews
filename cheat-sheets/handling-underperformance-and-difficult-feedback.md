# Handling Underperformance and Difficult Feedback Conversations

**Canonical chapter:** [`syllabus/19-leadership-staff/handling-underperformance-and-difficult-feedback.md`](../syllabus/19-leadership-staff/handling-underperformance-and-difficult-feedback.md)

## Core Mental Model

Withholding difficult feedback isn't kindness — it denies the person the specific information they need to actually improve, and lets a fixable problem compound.

## Essential Definitions

- **Pattern vs. single instance** — a genuine performance problem is the same specific issue recurring across multiple, distinct instances, not one bad sprint.
- **Observation vs. interpretation** — a specific, checkable fact ("missed the deadline") versus a story about why ("doesn't care") — lead with the former.
- **Follow-up** — a real, near-term check-in date; a conversation with no follow-up is functionally the same as no conversation.

## Decision Table

| Signal | Correct response |
|---|---|
| Single bad sprint, no prior pattern | Not yet a performance conversation — normal variation |
| Same specific issue, 3+ distinct instances | Real pattern — schedule a private, prompt conversation |
| Sudden change in a previously strong performer | Ask directly about external causes before assuming a skill/motivation problem |
| Same issue recurring across multiple team members | Check for a systemic cause before treating each as individual |

## Common Pitfalls

- Avoiding the conversation, hoping the problem resolves itself — the single most common and costly mistake.
- Leading with a character judgment instead of a specific, behavioral observation.
- Saving feedback for a scheduled review months later instead of addressing it promptly.
- Having the conversation once with no real follow-up date.

## Interview Answer Skeleton

**30-sec:** A genuine performance problem is a recurring pattern, not a single instance. Address it privately and promptly, leading with a specific, behavioral observation rather than a character judgment, and agree on a concrete follow-up date.

**2-min:** Add: separate observation from interpretation — ask what happened rather than asserting why, since the real cause is often different from what it looks like. A conversation with no follow-up is functionally no conversation at all.

**Staff-level framing:** An apparently individual performance problem sometimes has a systemic cause (unclear expectations, a broken estimation process) — fixing the system can matter more than addressing the one visible case.
