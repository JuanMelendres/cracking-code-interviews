# Hiring and Team Building

**Canonical chapter:** [`syllabus/19-leadership-staff/hiring-and-team-building.md`](../syllabus/19-leadership-staff/hiring-and-team-building.md)

## Core Mental Model

Structured interviewing means every candidate gets the same questions, scored against a shared, pre-decided rubric — a real, measured improvement over unstructured "gut feel" interviews (Google's own research). At Staff scope, team building means deliberately designing overall team composition, not just filling reqs one at a time.

## Essential Definitions

- **Structured interviewing** — same questions, shared rubric, every candidate — genuinely more predictive of on-the-job performance than unstructured interviews.
- **Calibration** — a recurring practice (not one-time) where interviewers score sample answers together to reduce scoring variance.
- **Anchoring bias** — the first debrief speaker's opinion disproportionately swaying the group; fixed by independent written assessments before discussion.
- **Culture add vs. culture fit** — hiring for a genuinely missing strength versus hiring for resemblance to the existing team.

## Decision Table

| Decision | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Structured interviewing with written rubrics | Measured improvement in predictive validity; consistent bar | Real upfront time to design and calibrate rubrics |
| Independent written assessments before debrief | Reduces anchoring bias | Slightly slower than immediate open discussion |
| "Culture add" over "culture fit" | Grows team's real range of perspective/capability | Real friction from working styles that don't automatically mesh |
| Explicit skill-gap-driven hiring | Each hire closes a real, named team weakness | Slower to fill a req than the first generically-qualified candidate |

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating the interview question list as the whole rubric — no written standard for what a strong versus weak answer contains.
- Skipping calibration for new interviewers, assuming shared context is automatic.
- Letting the first debrief speaker set the group's anchor instead of collecting independent assessments first.
- Filling a req with the easiest-to-find, most-resembles-the-team candidate instead of asking what specific gap the team needs filled.

## Interview Answer Skeleton

**30-sec:** Structured interviewing (same questions, shared rubric) measurably beats unstructured interviews; independent written assessments before debrief prevent anchoring bias; team building at Staff scope means designing composition deliberately, not just filling reqs.

**2-min:** Add: calibration is recurring, not one-time — new interviewers' scores measurably converge after a real calibration session using sample answers. "Culture add" (a genuinely missing strength) beats "culture fit" (resemblance) for growing real team capability.

**Staff-level framing:** The real Staff-level leverage is improving the hiring *process* and rubrics themselves — noticing a rubric fails to distinguish strong from weak candidates and revising it, or noticing a team's seniority mix has drifted unhealthy and advocating to correct it.
